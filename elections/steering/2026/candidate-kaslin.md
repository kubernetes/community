## What I have done

I’ve been a containers & Kubernetes enthusiast since 2014, involved in community groups since 2016, and finally found my way to long-term direct contribution in Kubernetes on SIG Contributor Experience’s Communications subproject starting in 2020.

I’ve now been a [co-lead of the Comms subproject since 2022](https://github.com/kubernetes/community/commit/f8a1459cc6787a3ef6bb912abd6de7e2b87ec525), and a [Co-Chair of SIG ContribEx since 2023](https://github.com/kubernetes/community/commit/36e9091305c2af6e14ce3b28adc8d84860e769ce).

I was honored to receive the [“Chop Wood, Carry Water” award in 2023](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2023/11/08/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-2023-community-awards-winners/).
I've also been a [CNCF Ambassador](https://www.cncf.io/people/ambassadors/?p=kaslin-fields&_sf_s=kaslin+fields) since 2019.

I’m passionate about leading the Kubernetes community to grow and thrive - focusing my community work on both growing contributors directly, and by creating programs and processes that ensure long term sustainability of the project.

Some of my proudest accomplishments in the community have been:

* **Running & driving the development and sustainability of the project’s social media presence across platforms** [since 2020](https://github.com/kubernetes/community/commit/ba8cbf2b64f063dc1f8f6fdb5e6006cb0bb3f00e), including creating community accounts on 5 platforms, leading the community takeover of the LinkedIn page and the 300k+ follower [Kubernetes.io](http://Kubernetes.io) X account in 2024, creating our KubeCon social program to enable new contributors to make a difference, and training up a new lead who has taken over during the last year!
* **Creating the [Kubernetes New Contributor Orientation](https://www.kubernetes.dev/docs/orientation/) (NCO) program** in 2024, reaching over 200 contributors live, including around 80 at our first in-person NCO at KubeCon EU, and ~6k views of YouTube recordings. This program is still running today (the third Tuesday of every month), and has developed a strong group of core contributors and leads who are driving a shift toward SIG-focused content to keep the program fresh and interesting.
* **Consistently running the weekly comms team meetings** for the last 4 years, and in so doing, welcoming and mentoring many new contributors into the project who have gone on to become leads in the comms team, in other sigs, and even in other CNCF projects.

## What I'll do

Y'know those moments at work where you're like "that's above my paygrade"? I like to think of the work steering does as being the "buck stops here" of the Kubernetes project.
I've asked steering members what they do plenty of times over the years, including in a [recorded interview for the Kubernetes Podcast from Google with Ben Elder](https://kubernetespodcast.com/episode/196-kubernetes-registry/), where he described it in a sentence as "It's just sort of a general escalation path for things."

I've run the communications team for years because I believe you can't solve a problem you can't see.
Through communications work, I've cultivated a broad view of the project.
New features, areas of the project that are the most understaffed, areas of risk to the project's infrastructure and overall health - all of it often comes through comms (or SIG ContribEx).
Solving the problems that make it to Steering requires a broad perspective, a willingness to learn, and the ability to drive collaborative solutions in the right areas of the project.

I’m running for Steering to help lead the community toward a sustainable future for Kubernetes, ensuring that as we continue to evolve, especially in this fast-paced era of AI-driven technologies, we remain a vibrant, contributor-first community where everyone can thrive.
