## What I have done

While earning my master's at DePaul, I led its Linux Community and user groups. I spent 13.5 years as a technical lead across several companies. I have contributed to Kubernetes for 10 years, beginning with [K8Guard](https://github.com/k8guard/k8guard-start-from-here), a security and auditing system. I spent most of that time leading **minikube**—millions' first Kubernetes experience, a daily driver for developers and CI systems, and users' favorite starting point in CNCF surveys. I rank among [Kubernetes' top all-time contributors](https://medya.dev/achievements), and minikube won a [2021 InfoWorld Bossie Award](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2270295/the-best-open-source-software-of-2021.html) under my leadership.

My work has lowered barriers to entry. I led [minikube's translation](https://minikube.sigs.k8s.io/docs/contrib/translations/) into more than [12 languages](https://github.com/kubernetes/minikube/tree/master/translations) despite pushback, so English fluency would not be required to get started. I optimized minikube for slow, restricted, or unreliable internet connections and supported the broadest range of runtimes, drivers, and platforms—minikube never picks a winner for you; you choose what fits your machine and your values. Accessibility is not a side feature to me; it is why minikube matters. This reflects my belief in the nature of open source: access without vendor boundaries.

I have mentored contributors from every continent and diverse backgrounds, helping them become maintainers. I contributed to tools that helped projects expand participation and recognize contributors, including [Triage Party](https://github.com/google/triage-party), which makes collaborative issue and pull-request triage easier. I invested in developer velocity through [gopogh](https://github.com/medyagh/gopogh). My nomination support comes from collaborators across organizations.

I will speak at **KubeCon + CloudNativeCon North America 2026** on “10 Years of Minikube and the Future of Millions of Users,” sharing minikube's leadership story.

## What I'll do

I am running as an independent candidate, not representing any company or employer. That perspective is useful on Steering: much of the project's daily work is carried by people whose community standing does not come with a large company behind it, and governance should reflect that.

If elected, I will focus on:

- **Transparency: clear, written rules.** Processes should be documented so first-time participants can follow them without knowing unwritten conventions. Rules should be findable, and discretionary enforcement should be visible.
- **Contributor accessibility and developer velocity.** Continuing my work in minikube by lowering barriers for contributors outside major vendors, across more languages and regions.
- **Sustaining maintainers.** Supporting the people doing unglamorous, long-term maintenance that keeps Kubernetes running and identifying tooling that improves developer velocity.
